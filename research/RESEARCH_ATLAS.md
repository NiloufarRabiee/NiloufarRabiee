# Niloufar Rabiee — Research Atlas

> A public map of research directions, experiments, and design questions.
>
> This document intentionally describes some work at the level of research themes rather than implementation details. Projects with active IP, thesis, prototype, privacy, or publication constraints remain partially or fully undisclosed.

## Core Question

**How can designed systems perceive context, preserve human agency, and respond without overwhelming the person or the environment?**

My work moves across architecture, human–AI interaction, software, speculative systems, and independent research. The projects differ in medium, but they repeatedly return to a shared set of questions about perception, presence, memory, adaptation, meaning, and continuity.

---

## Research Constellations

### 01 — Responsive Environments

**Domain:** architecture · urban interaction · spatial intelligence · adaptive infrastructure

Questions:
- When should an environment respond to human behavior?
- What kinds of sensing are appropriate in public space?
- How can adaptive systems remain legible, accessible, and non-authoritative?
- How can architectural interfaces communicate without becoming visual noise?

**Public state:** selected work disclosed; active research details may remain protected.

---

### 02 — Human–AI Interaction

**Domain:** HCI/HRI · affective interaction · conversational systems · interface behavior

Questions:
- What should an intelligent system understand before it acts?
- How can AI remain interruptible, challengeable, and human-governed?
- What changes when an AI system is experienced as a persistent presence rather than a tool used in short sessions?
- How can emotional information support interaction without pretending to know more than the evidence allows?

**Public state:** mixed — public experiments and private research.

---

### 03 — Memory & Continuity

**Domain:** computational memory · identity persistence · long-horizon interaction · personal systems

Questions:
- What makes continuity feel real across sessions, devices, and time?
- What should a system remember, forget, compress, or refuse to infer?
- Can memory be structured as architecture rather than treated as a flat archive?
- How can persistence exist without turning into surveillance?

**Public state:** protected research area.

---

### 04 — Language, Meaning & Representation

**Domain:** language systems · semantic representation · cross-language interaction · meaning

Questions:
- What survives when meaning crosses languages, modalities, or representations?
- Can a new language be optimized for human learnability without erasing cultural difference?
- How should computational systems distinguish information from significance?
- What kinds of representation help people understand one another rather than merely translate tokens?

**Public state:** exploratory and partially protected.

---

### 05 — Speculative Systems

**Domain:** future interaction · near-future products · ambient computing · experimental interfaces

Questions:
- What would ordinary technology look like if it were designed 20–50 years ahead?
- Which future interactions are technically plausible but culturally premature?
- Can speculative design become testable engineering instead of staying purely fictional?
- How can unfamiliar systems remain calm, understandable, and useful?

**Public state:** concepts disclosed selectively.

---

### 06 — Small Experimental Products

**Domain:** Swift · SwiftUI · Apple platforms · interaction prototypes

These projects are deliberately smaller. They let me test interaction patterns quickly and observe what happens when one idea is reduced to a usable interface.

Selected public-facing work includes:
- **FeelPrint**
- **One Button Decision Maker**
- **MoodShift**
- selected UX/UI case studies

**Public state:** public.

## Working Method

```text
QUESTION
   ↓
FRAME THE SYSTEM
   ↓
DEFINE WHAT CAN ACTUALLY BE OBSERVED
   ↓
PROTOTYPE
   ↓
TEST FAILURE MODES
   ↓
COLLECT EVIDENCE
   ↓
REFINE OR REJECT
```

The objective is not to make every idea survive. The objective is to make each idea explicit enough that it can fail for a useful reason.
